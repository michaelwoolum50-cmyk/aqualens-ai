# AquaLens AI — Demo Video Script (3–5 min)

## 0:00–0:30 — The problem
"Urban streams are the veins of our cities — but nobody's watching them. They face pollution,
algae blooms, and habitat loss, while monitoring is sparse and expensive. Citizen science can help,
but today's tools are jargon-heavy, observations are inconsistent, and raw AI verdicts can't be
trusted without human judgment. That's the gap AquaLens AI fills."

## 0:30–1:30 — Step 1 & 2: Photo + guided questions
"I'm standing at my local stream. I open AquaLens AI and snap a photo of the water. On-device
computer vision immediately reads color signals — green-dominant pixels and a vegetation index
for algae risk, dark-pixel ratio for turbidity. Nothing leaves my phone.
Then six plain-language questions — no science degree needed: How clear is the water? Any green
slime? Any trash? Any smell? Any wildlife? How's the flow? My answers ground every AI signal."

## 1:30–3:00 — Step 3: Explainable AI, human in the loop
"Here's the core innovation. The AI proposes findings — each with its reasoning shown and a
confidence score. Algae risk: the vision found 4.2% green pixels, and my answer agreed — high
confidence. Turbidity: moderate. Biodiversity: no visible life — a red flag.
But nothing is decided yet. I confirm, adjust, or reject EACH finding. The AI assists — I decide.
Only when every finding has my verdict does the Stream Health Index lock in: 58 out of 100 — AT RISK."

## 3:00–4:00 — Step 4: Dashboard + FHIR export
"My assessment is saved locally with a trend chart. And with one tap, I export a FHIR Observation —
the same data standard hospitals use — so researchers can ingest citizen data directly into real
health-data pipelines. That's Track 7 interoperability, built in."

## 4:00–4:30 — One Health impact + close
"Healthy streams mean healthy communities. AquaLens turns every citizen with a phone into a
verified environmental sensor — AI doing the heavy lifting, humans holding the final call.
From streams to systems: turning citizen science into actionable One Health intelligence.
Built for the OneAquaHealth IEEE Global Hackathon 2026, Track 3: AI-Supported Assessment."
